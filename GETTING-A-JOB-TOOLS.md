# Getting a Job — The Full Toolkit

I think about getting a job in 4 phases. Most people focus on one or two and neglect the rest. Here's what I've found works, with tools and advice for each.

---

## Phase 1: Finding the Job

Before you can apply, you need to know what's out there. I use a mix of:

### LinkedIn

Still the single best place to find roles. Set up job alerts for your target titles, follow companies you're interested in, and make sure your profile signals that you're open to opportunities (there's a setting for this that only recruiters can see).

### jackandjill.ai

A newer AI-powered job matching tool. Worth setting up alongside LinkedIn — it surfaces roles you might not find through traditional search.

### AI Job Search Tools

The landscape is evolving fast. Some tools worth looking at:

- **Jobright** — an AI job search copilot that matches you to roles and helps tailor applications
- **Teal** — job tracking, resume optimization, and cover letter generation in one place
- **Sonara / Loopcv** — automated job scanning across multiple boards

I haven't used all of these personally, so do your own research. The space moves quickly.

### Personal Networking

Still the most effective channel, especially for senior roles. Most jobs are never publicly advertised. Tell people you're looking — former colleagues, friends, people you've worked with. A warm introduction beats a cold application every time.

### Recruiters

If you're in a field with active recruiters (tech, finance, senior leadership), make sure a few good ones know you're on the market. They have access to roles you won't see on job boards.

## Phase 3: Passing the Interview

A friend shared an approach with me, and we've seen many people use it to land jobs. I've adapted it for working with this repo, and while it's not a secret sauce — you still need to put in the work — it will give you a genuine edge.

If you've used this repo for Phase 2, you already have a head start: the company research, tailored CV, and application notes in your `outputs/applications/` folder are the foundation. This phase is about turning that preparation into interview performance.

### The Approach

**Step 1: Know the company cold.** You've already got `company-research.md` and `application-notes.md` from Phase 2. Read through them properly. Then go further — ask AI to deep dive into the people who'll be interviewing you. Their LinkedIn, their background, what they care about. It's staggering how good AI is at this.

**Step 2: Practice with AI voice mode.** Use ChatGPT's Advanced Voice mode (on your phone) for mock interviews. Give it your CV, the job description, and your company research. Tell it to be aggressive and harsh — you want real critique, not encouragement. Do this multiple times (you get about 45 minutes a day). Actually listen to the feedback and adapt. If it says you waffle, fix it. If it says you need better examples, go back to your `achievements.md` and pick stronger ones. Trust it.

You can also do this with Claude, using the voice mode, but people like the more conversational way ChatGPT can be used (this does mean another subscription however).

**Step 3: Schedule interviews for late morning.** Aim for around 11am. Early enough that you're sharp, late enough that you've had time to do a full mock interview that morning.

**Step 4: Predict the questions.** Before the interview, ask AI to predict what questions will come up based on the role, the company, and who's interviewing you. Then practice those specific questions with voice mode. I estimate it gets about 80% of questions right.

**Step 5: Do a mock interview right before the real one.** This is the single most effective thing. Keep your examples and the feedback fresh in your mind.

**Step 6: Record the interview.** Use your phone's voice recorder app — not AI, just a plain recording. This is for your own learning. Check local laws on recording consent if you're unsure.

**Step 7: Get feedback on the transcript.** Feed the transcript back into AI. Ask for aggressive, honest feedback. What did you do well? Where did you ramble? What could be sharper?

**Step 8: Compound across rounds.** For round 2+, repeat all the above. Tell AI who's interviewing you next — their role, their level, their background. It'll generate bespoke questions and feedback tailored to that specific person. Each round makes the next one better.

### Why This Works

- You already have the research from Phase 2 — this turns it into muscle memory
- Gets you reps. Interview skills improve with practice like anything else
- AI gives honest feedback (friends are too nice)
- Keeps your best examples sharp and top of mind right before you need them
- Compounds across rounds — the feedback loop tightens every time

Everyone I know who's done this has said they felt more prepared going into interviews, regardless of the outcome.

## Phase 4: Starting Strong

You've got the offer — congratulations. But the job isn't done. The first 90 days set the tone for everything that follows.

### Before You Start

- **Negotiate properly.** Don't just accept the first offer. Research salary ranges for the role (Glassdoor, levels.fyi for tech, asking around). Even a small negotiation shows confidence and can make a meaningful difference.
- **Ask about onboarding.** What does the first week look like? Is there a buddy system? What tools and access will you need? Knowing this reduces first-day anxiety.
- **Connect with your new team.** Ask your manager if it's appropriate to introduce yourself to key teammates before day one. A quick LinkedIn message or email goes a long way.
- **Read up.** Company blog, recent press, annual reports, product docs — whatever is publicly available. You don't need to memorise it, but having context makes your first conversations much richer.
- **Sort out logistics.** Commute, parking, dress code, laptop setup, ID photos — get the boring stuff out of the way so day one is about people, not paperwork.

### Your First 30 Days — Learn

- Listen more than you talk. Ask questions. Take notes.
- Understand how decisions get made and who the key people are.
- Find out what success looks like for your role — ask your manager directly.
- Identify one small thing you can deliver or improve quickly. An early win builds credibility.
- Set up regular 1:1s with your manager if they're not already scheduled.

### Days 31–60 — Contribute

- Start taking on more responsibility. Volunteer for things.
- Build relationships outside your immediate team.
- Share observations — you have fresh eyes, and that perspective is valuable.
- Check in with your manager on how things are going. Don't wait for a formal review.

### Days 61–90 — Own

- You should be operating with increasing independence by now.
- Propose ideas, not just execute them.
- Think about what your 6-month and 1-year goals look like.
- Reflect on what's working and what isn't — and have that conversation with your manager.

### The General Principle

The people who start strong are the ones who are curious, proactive, and humble. You were hired because they believe in you — now show them they were right, while being genuinely open to learning how things work in this new context.

## A Note on AI Tools

This guide recommends using AI (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) throughout the process. A few things worth noting:

- **AI is a force multiplier, not a replacement.** It makes you more prepared, but you still have to show up and be yourself in the interview.
- **Use whatever tool you prefer.** This repo uses Claude, the interview prep section mentions ChatGPT's voice mode — use whatever works best for each task.
- **Keep it ethical.** AI helps you prepare better, not pretend to be someone you're not. Every fact on your CV should be true. Every interview answer should be genuinely yours.
- **The landscape changes fast.** The specific tools mentioned here may be outdated within months. The principles — research, practice, feedback, iterate — are timeless.
